# Chapter One: The Rhythm of the Beast

The city of Aethelgard did not stand on the earth; it rode it.

To live on the back of a god-sized turtle was to live on a planet that breathed. The ground beneath Kael’s boots was not solid, but the dense, fibrous muscle of the beast known only as the Anchor. When Kael walked, the floor of the lower market vibrated, a deep, subsonic thrum that he had felt in his teeth since birth. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.* It was not a heartbeat, the elders insisted, but the rhythm of the Anchor’s digestion, the slow grinding of continents.

Kael stopped his work. His wrench, a heavy piece of scrap iron forged in the Flanks, hung limp in his hand. He was a Rust-Singer, a worker in the Suture-Works where the city’s iron bones were welded to the beast’s skin. His job was to listen to the metal. If the iron groaned, the city would shift. If the skin blistered, the city would drown.

He pressed his palm against the cold iron piling. The vibration was wrong. It was too fast. Too erratic.

"Kael? You’re blocking the walkway."

He pulled his hand away, wiping the soot from the metal. "It’s shifting, Old Man."

Elder Maren, a figure draped in the grey wool of the Archives, stepped into the slanted light of a rusted arch. The old man’s face was a map of wrinkles, his eyes the pale, milky blue of a sky that had not seen rain in three generations. "The Anchor has been shifting for a thousand years, boy. It is how we survive. We are a parasite on a god, and parasites must adapt."

"Parasites don't get sick," Kael muttered, looking up. Above them, the underside of the great shell stretched out like a bruised horizon, a dome of matted fur and barnacle-crusted stone. The Shell was their roof, and today it looked like it was weeping black ichor.

A groan rolled through the Suture-Works, a sound like a ship’s hull creaking in a storm. The crowd of market-goers—women carrying baskets of kelp, children with faces smeared in grease—scattered.

Kael followed the sound. His job was to listen, but the sound was no longer a mere shift. It was a stutter. He followed it down the spiral stairs that wound into the dark belly of the city, where the Suture-Works began. Here, the air was thick with the smell of hot tar and ozone. The workers were stopped, their welding torches hissing out.

"Look at the seam," the Foreman, a towering woman named Bess, commanded.

Kael pushed through the crowd to the main piling. It was a massive column of iron, bolted to the Anchor’s hide, running the full length of the city. Right now, a hairline fracture was spreading across its surface, pulsing with a faint, sickly green light.

"That's impossible," Bess said, her voice tight. "Iron doesn't glow green. Green is the color of the Deep-Sickness."

Kael reached out, his hand trembling. He could feel the metal singing a high, thin note that made his teeth ache. "It's not the iron, Bess. It's the beast. The Anchor is trying to shake us off."

The ground lurched.

It was a violent, full-body shudder, so sudden and violent that Kael was thrown to his knees. The iron piling shrieked, a sound of tearing metal that echoed through the hollows of the city. From the upper levels, the sound of shattering glass began to ring out.

"Move!" Bess roared. "Everyone to the flanks! The Shell is cracking!"

Kael didn't move. He looked up, into the dark, and saw a light. Not the sickly green of the metal, but a brilliant, white starburst, far above the matted fur of the Shell. Something was happening in the sky. Or rather, something was happening to the sky.

The Anchor was not just a beast; it was a world. And for the first time in Kael’s life, he understood that the world was not infinite. It was merely waiting.

He stood up, his wrench forgotten in his hand, and turned toward the spiral stairs. He didn't know what was coming, but he knew the sound of the city breaking. He had to be there when it happened.

Above the Suture-Works, the great Shell of the turtle groaned, and a single, massive flake of its skin—the size of a market stall—detached and plummeted into the ocean below.

The city of Aethelgard was dying. And Kael was the only one who heard it scream.
