## final text

The sun leans low over the apple rows,
and my back aches with the day’s last weight.
I set the basket on the ground again,
its seams soft from a hundred plucked things.
The pickers murmur near the scale-house door,
where paper bags crinkle like small winds.
We measure hours in the coolness of the fruit,
in the thump of crates stacked one by one.
My hands are stained with red and yellow dust,
and my sleeves carry the orchard’s long day.
A truck idles at the end of the lane,
its engine humming a low goodbye note.
The boss taps the clipboard and says,
“We’ll leave it to the next pair of hands.”
I nod, because the words are true,
though the row behind me still leans toward me.
I reach once more into the heavy branch,
and pull a small apple from the dusk.
It is almost too red to hold,
as if it were a piece of the evening sky.
I taste the cold sugar on my tongue,
and remember the first hot morning I started.
Then the sky had been a clean blue cloth,
and the trees had whispered their heavy promise.
Now the leaves tremble with the coming dark,
and the birds have stitched themselves into silence.
I cap my hat with a tired hand
and brush the leaves from my forehead.
The crates are stacked, the truck is loaded,
and the path is clear where we once knelt.
My name is written in the payroll book
in the neat blue ink of a finished summer.
I look back at the orchard’s green maze,
and see myself small among the trees.
The light goes amber, then deep purple,
and the first stars prick the cooling air.
I put my hands in my pockets,
carrying only the taste of fruit and work.
The last day folds itself into the dark
like a leaf that finally lets go.
