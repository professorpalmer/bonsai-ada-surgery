## final text

The last sun climbs slow over the east fence
and I set my box of plums on the work table,
where every bruise I made this season
becomes a small blue map of where my hands were.

Forty crates sit stacked by the packing shed,
each one a Tuesday, a Wednesday, a Friday
when the sun was a coin I spent on the back
of my arms and the nape of my neck,
and the dust in the creases of my palms.

I pick up one more pear and hold it
to the open window of the shed. It is green
with a red kiss at its crown, the kind
that will be sweetest in November,
not today, not in my hands.

Maria waves from the road, her truck
already idling, engine a low hum
like a question I will not answer yet.

I walk the last row. The apples here
are bigger, slower to ripen. I press
my thumb into the side and feel the give,
the soft surrender of skin,
and I am not the girl who could do this
for sixteen hours and not think about her hands.

Someone left a glove in the dirt
between rows three and four. A child's glove,
too small for any of us who worked here,
the kind that belongs to a summer
that isn't this one.

The foreman calls my name and I turn.
He holds the book, the hours, the signature line.
"Same time next year?" He says it like
he's asking about the weather,
and I nod, and I sign.

I tip the last crate to the truck
and the plums spill over the edge,
and for a second the whole orchard
is just red, and wet, and gone.
The air smells like sap and the day before rain.

I walk out the gate.
I don't look back.
