## final text

The sun dips low behind the apple rows,
gilding the dust that settles on the ground.
I set the crate down, heavy with the weight
of every red and golden fruit I found.
My back aches for the long hours spent,
my fingers stained with sap and sun and time.
The pickers gather in a loose, tired ring,
trading stories that outlast the summer’s climb.
Elias wipes his brow with a worn hand,
saying, “There’s a song in the air we can’t name.”
I look at the truck parked at the tree line’s edge,
waiting like a promise I could claim.
We pour the last of the water in the pail,
the plastic clinking against the cool dirt.
The orchard holds its breath as the light fades,
a secret kept in the dark of the dirt.
I leave behind the scent of crushed skin,
the sweet rot of a fruit left too long.
It’s the end of the season, the end of the day,
and I carry the taste of the summer home.
