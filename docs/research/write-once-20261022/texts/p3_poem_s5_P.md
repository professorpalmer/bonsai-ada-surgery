## final text

The peach tree is bare of fruit and bare of light.
We stand in the last rows, our hands still tight
Against the wood, as if we can pull out
The rest of the sun before it sinks out.
The boss calls out the hours, a final bell,
And the silence that follows is a heavy spell.
I check the pockets of my apron and shirt,
Where the dust of the orchard has settled and hurt.
It is a fine, white powder that stays in the skin,
A map of the summer, a fine, bitter stain.
The partner leans on the fence and looks up,
At the sky that is turning into a cup
Of bruised and soft purple, a dying bruise.
We do not speak of the days that we refuse
To remember, the days where the work was a song,
Where the fruit was so sweet that the labor felt wrong.
But now the work is done, and the song is gone.
We are just two figures in a long row.
The tractor sits idle, its engine gone cold,
And the shadows of the trees stretch long and old
Across the packed earth where we walked all day.
There is nothing to pick and there is nothing to pay.
Just the heat that is lifting off the red dirt,
And the feeling of a life that has been hurt
By the labor of it, but also made whole
By the hours of the work and the hours of the soul.
I pack up the basket and the knife and the bag,
And we walk back to the truck with a heavy lag
In the legs and a headache that thuds in the skull.
The day is over, the fruit has been full.
We start the engine, the seat creaks and groans,
And the orchard disappears in the distance and bones.
The road is long, and the heat is a ghost.
There is no more fruit, there is no more most.
We drive through the dust, and the dust is the end,
Of the last day of summer, a quiet, deep end.
