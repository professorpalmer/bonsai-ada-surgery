# Bonsai 2 small models on the 27B serving stack: 9B, 4B and 2B complete (2026-10-07, early-access report, published after the release)

Setup: RTX 4070 12 GB, Windows, the Bonsai serving stack (PrismML fork `adfffbe` + the 33 serving patches, the same
engine as the public 27B bundle), one slot, full 262,144 window at q8_0 K/V entirely in VRAM, all layers on the GPU,
no draft head (none of the small files has one). Every number below is greedy unless stated, plans were written
before results, and the per-sample receipts are in the repo for anyone who wants to re-score.

Arms:
- **raw**: the GGUF's own template and defaults, presence 0, no reasoning budget, no harness flags.
- **raw+pp**: the same with `--presence-penalty 1.5` (Tester A's loop finding).
- **serve**: our recipe: medium effort, reasoning budget 20,480 with a forced close, harness-proofing (effort words
  normalized, small output caps raised), backend sampling, presence 0.
- **serve+pp**: the same with presence 1.5. The layer (API cards, API check, sandboxed Python) rides on the serve arm
  for the suite.

## 9B PTQ1_0

**Speed and memory.** 7.1 GB at load with the whole 262k window resident; decode 166.6 / 143.1 / 100.9 / 72.3 tok/s at
0 / 16k / 64k / 128k of context (mean over code, prose and bash prompts, 400 tokens each). Partial offload is not an
option (see the loader note posted earlier); full offload is.

**HumanEval 164, pass@1, sandbox-scored, 24,576-token cap at medium.** Cap hits per arm: see the correction below.

| arm | thinking off | medium (thinking on) | medium wall time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| raw | 133 | 139 | 85 min |
| raw + presence 1.5 | 128 | **152** | 50 min |
| serve (budget + forced close) | 133 | **151** | 80 min |
| serve + presence 1.5 | 128 | **153** | 54 min |

Reading: at medium, presence 1.5 and the budget-plus-forced-close each recover about 12 problems over raw, and the
two overlap almost completely (together: one more, inside noise). The penalty also cuts thinking time by ~40%. With
thinking off the penalty costs 5 problems on both arms. Raw at medium ran 16 problems to the cap; with either lever
0 or 1 did, so most of the gain is runaway thinking stopped (Tester A's loop finding, here on code).

**Correction (2026-10-07): cap hits.** An earlier version of this report said that no HumanEval run hit the
24,576-token cap. That was wrong: the battery's loop counter read a field that the sample file does not have, so it
always printed 0. The real counts, from each arm's `summary.json`:

| HumanEval runs that hit the cap (thinking on, 24,576) | 9B | 4B | 2B |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| raw | **16** | 6 | 9 |
| raw + presence 1.5 | 1 | 0 | 2 |
| serve (budget + forced close) | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| serve + presence 1.5 | 0 | 0 | 1 |

So on the 9B most of the medium gain (139 -> 151-153) is runaway thinking that the penalty or the budget stops:
16 problems ran to the cap raw, 0-1 with either lever. This agrees with tester A's loop finding. With thinking off the
cap is 1,024 tokens and 3-6 answers per arm reach it (long answers, not loops).

**Tool calls** (9 requests per arm, native Qwen3-Coder XML with the server grammar vs JSON asked for in content):
grammar 9 of 9 parsed (payload fully correct on 5), JSON-in-content 1 of 9 with 3 runs cut by the cap. Same shape as
the 27B: send `tools`, let the server format them.

**Long exact-work suite** (37 tasks: coding 12, computation 15, workspace 10; frozen plan, same seeds; raw vs behind
the layer): **9 / 37 vs 9 / 37**, 2 rescues and 2 losses, the layer at 0.52x the completion tokens.
Coding 0/12 on both arms. Computation 6/15 both (`digits` and `weblog` +1 with the layer, `sales` -2; `knapsack` 3/3
both, `lcs` 0/3 both). Workspace 3/10 both (`copy` 2/2, `chain` 1/2, the three harder items 0/2: a patch is
submitted every time, the full test fails).

Reading: the layer, which took the 27B from 17 to 28 on this suite, is a wash on the 9B. Its sandbox only helps
when the model writes the computation as code and reads `input.txt` instead of retyping data; the 9B does that
inconsistently. The exact-work families are mostly beyond a 9B regardless of how it is served.

**What I'd put on the 9B card:** medium thinking by default; `--presence-penalty 1.5` recommended **with thinking
on** (and left at 0 with thinking off); a reasoning budget with a forced-close message as an alternative that does the
same job without touching sampling; native tool calling via `tools`; full GPU offload required.

## 4B PTQ1_0 (HumanEval grid complete, serve-arm suite running)

| arm | thinking off | medium | medium wall time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| raw | 117 | **148** | 41 min |
| raw + presence 1.5 | 125 | 146 | 31 min |
| serve + presence 1.5 | 125 | 146 | 36 min |
| serve | 117 | **152** | 41 min |

Reading so far: the 4B gains 31 problems from thinking (the 9B gained 6), and lands within three of the 9B at medium.
The penalty's medium gain seen on the 9B is absent here (148 vs 146, noise), and with thinking off its sign flips:
+8 on the 4B where it was -5 on the 9B. So presence 1.5 is not a blanket recommendation; it is a per-size setting.
Cap hits at medium: raw 6, every other arm 0.

**4B suite** (37 tasks, raw vs behind the layer): **7 / 37 vs 12 / 37**, 6 rescues and 1 loss, the layer at 0.84x
the tokens. Coding 0/12 both; computation 4/15 -> 9/15; workspace 3/10 both. Unlike the 9B, the layer's sandbox lifts
the 4B on exact computation. Tool calls: grammar 8 of 9 parsed, JSON-in-content 1 of 9.

**4B reading:** the budget + forced close is the setting that helps at medium (152, the best 4B score); presence 1.5
does nothing there and helps only with thinking off. So the per-size card advice differs: 9B wants presence 1.5 with
thinking on, the 4B wants the budget and no penalty.

## 2B PTQ1_0

| arm | thinking off | medium | medium wall time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| raw | 69 | 88 | 16 min |
| raw + presence 1.5 | 66 | **95** | 9 min |
| serve + presence 1.5 | 66 | **95** | 11 min |
| serve | 69 | 88 | 15 min |

Reading: thinking is worth 19 problems on the 2B. Here presence 1.5 is the lever at medium (+7, as on the 9B) and the
budget + forced close adds nothing. Cap hits at medium: raw 9, raw+pp 2, serve and serve+pp 1. Tool calls: grammar 8 of 9 parsed, JSON-in-content 2 of 9.

**2B suite** (37 tasks, raw vs behind the layer): **0 / 37 vs 3 / 37**, 3 rescues and no losses (`digits` +1,
`knapsack` +2). The exact-work suite is below the 2B's floor; the sandbox buys a few computation wins.

**Per-size advice, all three:** 9B presence 1.5 with thinking on; 4B the reasoning budget and no penalty; 2B
presence 1.5 with thinking on. Thinking on at every size (+6 / +31 / +19 problems).

**Test-first experiment (from Killy's plates).** We added one sentence to coding requests: write a failing test
from the specification first, then implement. Coding family, 12 paired runs per size. Result: 0 of 12 with and
without the sentence, on the 9B, the 4B and the 2B. The sentence works as an instruction (the 9B wrote a test file
in 12 of 12 runs against 3 of 12). But the most frequent failure is the result format, and the model's own tests do
not catch it, because the model writes the tests from the same incorrect reading of the specification. Almost all
runs on both arms also used all 24 agent steps. This agrees with Killy's finding: the small models fix code when a
correct failing test exists, and they cannot make that test themselves.

Teacher (Qwen3.5 Q4_K_M) batteries: running. First number: the 9B teacher with thinking off gets 137 of 164 (the
1-bit 9B gets 133).
