## final text

The supervisor says the trucks leave by four,
and I nod because my shoulders have said
yes all along the way without my knowing,
and the light is already gone gold.

I take the last row, the long one at the north
end where the apples lean together like
old friends deciding who will break first,
who will hold on to the season.

My hands remember the twist now. Stem
gives way, and the fruit drops into my palm
heavy as a small, cold argument.
I have picked ten thousand. This is one.

The basket is a green thing at my side,
and I pour into it what I can,
and the juice runs down the cut end
of the stem, sweet, a slow red map.

By noon the rows are silent as a church.
A crow works a patch of sun on the dirt.
My back is a thing I have to think about.
I set the basket down and stand and breathe.

The last crate, the last one on the truck,
I stack it, and the driver gives a nod,
and I know that this is how it ends—
not with a door, but with a load.

My fingernails hold the dirt and the pith
of the stem. I scrub them at the spigot
and the water runs pink and then just
clear, and the orchard watches me scrub.

I fold my hat and set it on the truck.
The cab is hot. The windows are down.
I can smell the diesel and the warm oil
and the particular green after the picking stops.

The rows stretch back, unclaimed, into the heat,
and the sun sits on the ground like a stone
that has been warm all day and will be warm
without me, in the dark, on the farm.

I turn the key. The engine coughs, then starts.
The road pulls the orchard out behind the glass,
and the summer goes, and goes,
and goes.
