## final text

The last basket sits on the truck's tailgate,
Heavy with plums, the pit still cool inside,
And I unlace the boots I wore all day—the
Left one stiff with sap, the right one scored
By a thorn I didn't see until the gate
Was closing on the path where I had been.

The orchard stretches out, a thousand rows
Of apple trees that hold the light like cups
Full to the rim of August. Every row
I walked this morning, row by row, is gone
Behind me now, a green and amber sea
I'll not be picking from again by night,
And the light is going soft, and gold, and thin.

The foreman nods. I nod. We say the words—
"Good work this season." "Thanks for coming back."
The words are flat. They're not the kind of words
That need a thank-you back, but still I say
them, because the mouth must move when hands
Are empty for the first time in three weeks,
And the silence after is its own small ache.

I peel the tape off my wrist, the red
Of sunburn, and the dirt in the nails—
That's the last thing I'll take from here, that red,
That black beneath the cuticle. It'll be
Scrubbed out by Monday, maybe. By then
I'll be somewhere with a different sun,
And this dirt will be a ghost in the sink.

The birds are doing their late-summer thing,
A chorus in the upper canopy,
As if the trees are saying something true
About the weight of having held the fruit
And set it down. The crows begin to drop
From branch to branch like dark, small decisions.

I lift my basket one last time and feel
The plums shift, settle, press against my hip.
A summer ends. The gate swings shut behind
The sound of crows and the long green hum
Of leaves that don't know they've been counted.
I put my keys in the lock. The truck bed
Holds its cargo. The air is dirt and gone.
