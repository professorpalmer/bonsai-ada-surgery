## final text

The last afternoon leaned like a ripe apple
over rows of orchard green,
where we had split our fingers to the bone
and counted every fruit unseen.
The sun hung low behind the barn,
a peach still warm in every palm,
and my shirt was stitched with red dust,
a map of all the summer’s demand.
We passed the ladder and the crate,
the last small bowl of sour grapes,
the cooler’s lid still fogged with breath,
the smell of juice upon the stairs.
Old Tom, who knew the trees by name,
wiped sweat from each one’s face,
and said, the orchard keeps its time
and gives the sweetest back at pace.
We stacked the bins beneath the shed,
and heard the crows begin to call,
the way the wind would through the leaves
when August starts to turn its fall.
My back was bent beneath the load,
my tongue was dry with sun and dust,
but I kept finding, fruit by fruit,
the shape of summer in the chest.
The truck engine coughed once, twice,
then sputtered into evening blue,
and I leaned on the tailgate’s edge,
looking out across the orchard’s hue.
The rows ran dark behind the fence,
the apples heavy on the bough,
the last bright sun touched every trunk
and made the green like a crown.
We did not speak of what to do
when the truck would pull away,
when the fields would sleep and the road
would take us far from the day.
But there was joy in the work’s end,
in the cool wind off the creek,
in the promise of next August,
when the fruit would sweeten with the week.
