## final text

I will write the poem directly, keeping each line distinct and counting to exactly 40.

The orchard hums a low and golden tune
as August light slants low across the rows,
where every apple turns a rusted moon
toward the sun that no one asks to go.
My gloves are stiff with juice, with dust, with rain
that came and went in sudden silver sheets,
a summer’s work half done, a half-remain
in the green hush where the bees still greet
the blossoms that are gone, the leaves that stay
in copper, amber, faintly bruised with age.
I trace the seams where fruit met sun and spray
and feel the quiet of a working page
that closes without ink, without a name.
The crate is heavy. So is the day.
So is the word I almost say.
The truck sits low beside the gravel line,
its bed still wet from yesterday’s design
of picking, sorting, breathing, and the climb
through rows that knew my boots by name.
A crow moves once. A branch releases fruit
onto the soft and waiting, leafed-up ground.
I stand a moment. Do I need to?
The summer holds its breath and waits around.
The last pick is a small, imperfect red,
its skin dimpled, sweet, a little thin.
I put it in the box I’ll never feed
with any other fruit that will not begin
to ripen in the dark of winter’s shelf.
The sky turns pale. The light goes through the trees.
I hear the gate. I hear the truck’s last shelf
of sound as it begins to hum with ease.
The sun is low. The orchard knows I’m late.
My hands still carry what the days have made.
I drive through fields where the whole world is weight
and light and leaf and something I can’t state.
Behind me, summer folds its quiet close
like cloth on a table after a long meal.
No one sees how much the work discloses:
the shape of hands, the taste of what I feel.
I leave with dirt beneath each nail and skin.
The road takes me out of the fruit’s wide grin.
