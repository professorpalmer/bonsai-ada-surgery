## final text

The gate still holds the lock I broke in June
with a rusted key that stuck like a splinter.
I push through to the first row where the pears
hang heavy and yellow, and the air is sweet
with the green rot of overripe fruit.
My back aches before I've lifted the basket.
The sun is already doing its work
on my neck, the way it did each day—
a slow, patient press that never once
asked whether I was still there.

The crew's already out past the third row,
their boots making the small percussion of work.
I pick a pear, turn it over in both hands,
feel the bruise where a June storm bent the branch.
One good. The next, soft as a held breath.
I drop it where it lands. The basket swells.
Marcus shouts a line I don't catch—
something about the last pallet, the truck
coming at four. We fill the bins
and the afternoon goes thin and long.

At three I set the basket down
and look at what my hands have taken:
a hundred, maybe two, of them—
not the number the pay envelope cares for,
just the weight. I feel it in the knuckles,
in the shoulder that has rounded
into a question mark all summer.
I drink from the jug, the water
cold and flat and mine, and the row
stretches out with nothing left in it.

The truck idles in the parking strip.
I carry my last basket to the bed
where the others pile their days
into a small, sweet mountain.
The orchard behind me is a green hush.
The leaves are already turning their backs
to the sun. I climb the running board
and the engine shakes the road out
into dust. I don't look back.
I just feel the seat hold me like a hand.
